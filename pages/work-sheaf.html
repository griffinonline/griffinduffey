a calm front end for ai coding agents

sheaf my own product · designer + builder 2026 sheaf.to

the problem

ai coding agents live in a terminal, on one machine, in one window. i do most of my thinking away from a desk: on my phone, on a walk, on a laptop that goes to sleep. i wanted to start a job, close the lid, and come back to finished work.

the tools that existed were either a raw terminal on a phone or a dashboard built for engineers. i wanted something calm enough to use all day.

what i did

  • a server on my own computer. bun and typescript on an old macbook that's always on at home. chats, docs and history live there, never in the browser.
  • work that keeps going. each chat runs as its own background service, so it survives the app restarting, a deploy, or my laptop closing.
  • everything next to the chat. terminals for both machines side by side, dev server previews, docs, calendar, and long jobs that tell you when they're done.
  • four simple ideas. areas for the long-running parts of life and work, chats for the task in front of you, docs for what you keep, machines so a home server, laptop and phone work as one.
  • a calm look. off-white paper, black ink, hairline rules, light and dark. no dashboards, no uppercase labels.
  • a real release. a signed and notarized mac app, a linux build, installs that update themselves, and a one-line "ship it" that tests, builds and publishes a new version.

how it went

it's been my daily driver since the first week: i'm writing this case study from inside it. about 380 commits in under three weeks, from first commit to a closed beta.
